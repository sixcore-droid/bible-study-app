How the Study Workstation works

Three parts: the reading plan on the left, the reader in the middle, and the side panel on the right, opened from the buttons down the right edge.

Something broken, or something you'd like it to do? Report a bug Ask for a feature

How to use it

The top bar

Along the top, left to right.

  1. Plan Shows or hides the reading plan on the left (on a phone it slides in).
  2. jayms.com Opens the online version of the app on jayms.com.
  3. Bible Study / Hebrew & Greek / Atlas Switches between the three. Hebrew & Greek reads John, Genesis and Psalms with the places the original shapes the English marked. Atlas is the map on its own.
  4. Atlas The map full size with no reader, and a search: a place, a journey, a kingdom, or a topic like the Ark (where it went, and every place it is claimed to be today, with the case for and against). Opening a verse goes back to Bible Study at that passage.
  5. Classes Your online classes (Peterson Academy, BibleProject Classroom, AWKNG): a lesson plays in its own window on the right half of the screen, you tick it Done and keep notes here, and a semester plan lays the lessons left in every class onto the days you choose.
  6. Learn Packs to learn from, read in place from your vault. The first is Reading Greek Deeply: every lesson, with each Greek word's lemma beside it (click a word and it says its lemma), a paradigm opened from any code, vocabulary and paradigm drills, and a review of what you have finished.
  7. My desk / Read along My desk is your full view. Read along shows what a visitor sees.
  8. Settings Turns Logos links and verse numbers on or off.
  9. About Why this app exists.
  10. Donate Buy Me a Coffee, to support the project.
  11. Help This guide, plus the buttons to report a bug or ask for a feature.

The reading plan

The left side: which plan you're reading and where you are in it.

  1. Reading plan Tap to pick a plan. Each app lists its own plans.
  2. The Bible Go to any book, chapter and verse without changing your plan.
  3. Matching psalm Adds a psalm that matches the day's reading.
  4. Hide genealogies & lists Genealogy and list chapters are greyed in the list; this takes them out.
  5. Progress Which day of the plan it is, and how much is done.
  6. Restart this plan Clears this plan's Done ticks and starts it again from day 1, today. It asks first, and your other plans are not touched.
  7. A day Tap a day to read it. The list starts at the first day not yet done.
  8. Done tick Ticks itself when every reading that day is marked Done.

The reader

The middle: the passage, and how it's shown.

  1. Reading tabs One tab for each of the day's readings, and one for each passage you open with Go. × closes an opened passage.
  2. Go to a passage Type any reference, like Mark 13:14 or Psalm 82, and press Go. It opens in its own tab beside the day's readings.
  3. Find in the text Highlights a word everywhere in the passage on screen. Enter jumps to the next one. It sits beside Go to a passage; in a narrow window it folds to a search button.
  4. Open in Logos Opens the passage in your Logos app.
  5. Verses / Reading Named for what it switches to: one verse per line, or paragraphs.
  6. Parallel / Single Shows two versions side by side, paragraph by paragraph.
  7. Hide # / Show # Takes the verse numbers off the page or puts them back (also in Settings).
  8. Version Which translation you read in: LSB, ESV, NET, NLT, NASB, CSB, BSB, KJV, MSG or Brenton's Septuagint.
  9. A− and A+ Makes the text smaller or bigger.
  10. Voice Speechify voice and speed for Read. It shows once you have pasted your own Speechify key in Settings.
  11. « » Previous and next chapter.
  12. FC Full chapter: in place of « » when only part of a chapter is on screen, with a breadcrumb back to the verses you were on.
  13. Verse number Tap a verse number for that verse's tools.
  14. Highlight and Read Highlight marks a column and copies it; Read reads it aloud with Speechify (once your own key is in Settings).

Under the text

When you've read it. On jayms.com, James's public notes on the passage show under the text when he has written some, and the readings you mark Done are kept in your own browser on that device, so nobody else sees them.

  1. Done Marks this reading done; the day ticks when all its readings are.
  2. My notes Your note on this passage. It saves to your notes and can open in Obsidian.
  3. Public Lets visitors on jayms.com read this note.
  4. Save note Saves the note (Cmd+S works too).
  5. Close Folds the notes bar down to its header, or brings it back. Drag its top edge to make it taller or shorter.

Down the right edge (along the bottom on a phone). Each opens a panel about the passage on screen. A number is how many there are for this passage, a greyed button has nothing for it, and a plain one always has something.

  1. Verse Verse tools: one verse in Hebrew or Greek word order with Strong's numbers, Dead Sea Scrolls readings, and any words in your glossary. Tap a word in the grid and its Word Study card opens right there under the grid (Strong's, meaning, lexicon, where it appears, + Vocab); tap it again or Close to fold it, Word Study to open the full panel.

A panel

What opens when you tap a side button.

  1. Heading What the panel is showing, for which passage.
  2. × Closes the panel. Tapping the same side button closes it too.
  3. Edge Drag the panel's edge to make it wider or narrower; double-click puts it back.
  4. Boxes Each box's coloured edge says what it is: gold is Scripture, green is an ancient witness, olive is a reading of it, blue is an open question.

Hebrew & Greek

The same frame, reading John, Genesis or Psalms with the places the original shapes the English marked.

  1. Hebrew & Greek Switches to this app; Bible Study switches back. Each remembers its own plan.
  2. A marked word An underlined word is a place the English Bibles part company. Red changes the claim, gold the emphasis, grey is cosmetic.
  3. The original word The Hebrew or Greek word, in English letters, right after the word it explains. Hover either and both light up; tap to open it.
  4. Marks to show Under the plan: Original words shows or hides the tags; the colour boxes turn each kind of mark on or off.
  5. Marks The panel for the mark you tapped: the versions around it, then plain English, what you're seeing, why translators split and the rule to carry away. ‹ › or the arrow keys step through the chapter's marks.
  6. ‹ › Steps to the previous or next mark in the chapter (the arrow keys do too).

The same app, narrower.

Every button

The buttons down the side (along the bottom on a phone): a number is how many there are for this passage; a greyed button has nothing for this passage; a plain one always has something to show.

Hebrew & Greek

The second switch in the top bar. It is "Hebrew and Greek Without Learning Either" from jayms.com, read a chapter at a time in this same frame. The plans are John, Genesis and Psalms, a chapter a day.

Marks
An underlined word is a place where the English Bibles part company. The tag right after it is the Hebrew or Greek word, in English letters. Red changes the claim, gold changes the emphasis, grey is cosmetic. Hover one and its word and tag light up together; tap either to open it.
The Marks panel
The versions, cut to the words around the mark (Whole verse shows all of it), then in plain English, what you are seeing, why translators split, and the rule to carry away. ‹ › or the arrow keys step from mark to mark.
The boxes under the plan
Original words shows or hides the tags. The three colour boxes turn each kind of mark on or off; Cosmetic starts off.

The top bar

Plan
Shows or hides the reading plan (on narrow screens).
Go to a passage
Type any reference, like Mark 13:14 or Psalm 82, and press Go.
Find in the text
Finds a word in the passage on screen and highlights every match. Enter goes to the next match, Shift+Enter back, Esc clears it. Ctrl+F or Cmd+F jumps here.
Read along / My desk
One button, named for the view it switches to. My desk is your full view. Read along shows what a reader would see: the text, public notes and the public panels only. In Read along, James's notes sit in the same bar along the bottom of the reader: Close and Open it, or drag its top edge to resize.
Menu (phone)
On a phone there is no top bar. The round button at the top right opens a menu with everything it holds: Reading (the reading plan, The Bible), Text size (A− and A+, with the size shown; the menu stays open while you tap), Apps (Bible Study, Hebrew & Greek, Atlas) and More (My desk or Read along, Add to Home Screen, Settings, About, Donate, Help). Add to Home Screen installs the app with its own icon: on Android it brings up the phone's install prompt, on an iPhone it shows the two taps in Safari (Share, then Add to Home Screen); once installed it leaves the menu. Tap outside it to close it. Go to a passage and Find in the text are left off on a phone; The Bible does that job.
Settings
Checkboxes, in groups. Reading: Reading plan (off takes the plan away: no plan list, days or progress on the left, no day's tabs, no Done or Next; The Bible stays as an open book picker, and the reader shows just the passages you open, each with a tab you can close; turn it back on and the plan is where you left it), The Bible button (shows or hides it under the reading plan) and Verse numbers (off takes them off the page, so the passage reads straight through and Speechify skips them). Colours in the text: Words of Jesus in red (from the World English Bible's red letters; where only part of a verse is his, the words inside his quotation marks), then on your desk Old Testament connections in blue (verses with a note in the CSB Connecting Scripture New Testament) and Jesus using the Old Testament in purple (his words inside those verses); each has its own checkbox, so any mix works, and Matthew 4 shows all three. Logos: Logos links are every link into Logos Bible Software (Open in Logos under the title, the commentaries and study Bibles on the Shelf, the atlases under Maps); in Read along they go through Logos's own web links, so a book you own opens and one you don't opens its page on logos.com. Interleaved Bible (phone): English, Hebrew or Greek, and Lemma, each line under the words on or off; on a computer they are buttons in the Interleave panel. Read aloud (Speechify): paste your own Speechify API key (from platform.speechify.ai) and the Voice and Read buttons appear; the key stays in this browser and goes only to Speechify, so treat it like a password; Speechify's free plan covers about 40 chapters a month; Remove takes it out. Below the key, Voice and speed: pick the voice that reads, and the speed from 0.5x to 2x (1x is normal). The same two controls are under the Voice button on the text. This browser remembers all of them.
Help
This guide.

A refresh brings you back to where you were: the plan, the day and reading (or the passage you opened, until midnight), the panel you had open, and how far down the page you were.

Reading plan (left)

Reading plan

Matching psalm
Adds a psalm to each day that fits that day's chapter, with the reason it was picked.
Genealogies & lists
Genealogy and list chapters stay in the plan, set back in grey and marked "genealogy / list"; the list never starts on one. Tick Hide genealogies & lists to take them out of the list.
Days
The list shows the first day not yet done and the six after it; Show all opens the rest, with the days already done at the bottom from day 1. Tap a day to read it. Its box ticks itself once every reading that day is marked Done. The bar shows how far through the plan you are.
Days
Tap a day to read it. A plan starts at day 1 the first day you open it, and moves on a day at a time.

Highlight

A− and A+
Text size. On a phone it is in the round menu instead.

Verse numbers

Dotted words

Done and Next

My notes
Your notes on this passage. Tick Public to let readers see them. Save note keeps them; once saved, Open in Obsidian goes to the note in your vault. The notes sit in a bar along the bottom of the reader, so they stay in view while you read: Close folds it down to its header, Open brings it back, and dragging its top edge makes it taller or shorter (double-click the edge to reset). The app remembers both. On your desk, Save note also writes the note into your Obsidian vault (02-BIBLE/Study Workstation) straight away; any saved note not yet in the vault is written there when the app starts, and a note you have since edited in Obsidian is left alone. It works the other way too: when the app starts, any note in that folder that is newer than the app's copy (written or edited in Obsidian) loads into My notes. If you save in the app after the Obsidian copy changed, the Obsidian copy is kept and your edit is saved beside it as an app copy. A saved note shows formatted, the way Obsidian shows Markdown (headings, bullet lists, bold, quotes, tables); Edit, or a tap on the note, opens the plain editor, and Formatted or Save note goes back. James's notes in Read along and the Notes page are formatted the same way. The bar's heading shows ✓ saved when this passage has a saved note, even with the bar closed.

Run your own copy

You can run this app on your own computer and plug in your own study tools. Each one is optional.

What it adds

  • Logos: your commentaries and study Bibles on the Shelf, each opening in Logos at the passage.
  • Obsidian: your notes on each passage saved to your vault, and the notes you already have that mention it.
  • Speechify: the Read button reads the passage aloud.
  • Claude: ask about the passage in your own claude.ai Project.

Set it up

  1. Get Python 3 On a Mac, type python3 in Terminal and follow the prompt. On Windows, install it from python.org.
  2. Download the app (zip) and unzip it.
  3. Open a terminal in that folder On a Mac, type cd and a space in Terminal, then drag the folder in and press Return. On Windows, right-click the folder and choose Open in Terminal.
  4. Answer the questions Type python3 setup/setup.py. Press Enter to skip any you don't use. It finds Logos on its own.
  5. Start it Type python3 setup/serve.py, then open http://localhost:8940/ in Chrome or Safari. Leave the terminal open while you study.

What each one asks for

Logos
Nothing to type. Open Logos once first so its library list is current. Bought more books? Run python3 setup/setup.py --refresh.
Obsidian
The vault's name, as Obsidian shows it, and its folder (drag it in). Optional: a second vault of books, one folder per author, for the Shelf's Vault tab.
Speechify
An API key from Speechify's developer page. It stays on your computer and only goes to Speechify.
Claude
The link to a claude.ai Project you made for Bible study.
Podcasts
Nothing needed. Your own Podcast Index key is optional.

Keeping it current

Download the zip again for a newer version. Copy your setup/settings.json and setup/mine folder into it, then run python3 setup/setup.py --refresh. Your answers and keys never leave those two places. The full guide is in setup/README.md.
